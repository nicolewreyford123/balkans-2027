All three, in some order

All three countries are in — that isn't up for debate. What's open is the order we pass through them and which gateways the flights make sensible. Every option below visits Croatia, Bosnia & Herzegovina and Montenegro; none is a plan, and none comes with a day-by-day. They're here to be priced, in whatever order the fares rank them.

The Flights & visas page now carries the first real fares, checked on Google Flights in November 2026: Johannesburg is the cheaper departure by roughly R6,000, and Zagreb and Podgorica are the cheapest gateways found so far at about R13.5k return. Those are single-city returns, though — the open-jaw pairs that decide these six orders are still unpriced.

  • 1 · Croatia → Bosnia & Herzegovina → Montenegro — north to south — Zagreb (Upper Town, St Mark's, Dolac) → Sarajevo (Baščaršija, the Tunnel of Hope, Vrelo Bosne) → Konjic and Mostar (Stari Most, Blagaj, Počitelj, Kravica falls) → Dubrovnik (walls, Srđ) → Kotor Bay, Perast and Lovćen. In ZAG, out Tivat (TIV) or Podgorica (TGD) — a Montenegrin exit is what keeps this one directional. The inland-to-coast spine.
  • 2 · Bosnia & Herzegovina → Croatia → Montenegro — inland, then the Dalmatian coast — Sarajevo (4 nights: Baščaršija, the Tunnel of Hope, Vrelo Bosne) → Konjic and Mostar (4: Stari Most, Blagaj, Počitelj, Kravica falls, rafting) → Split (the palace, Marjan, an island day) → Dubrovnik (walls, Srđ) → Kotor Bay, Perast and Lovćen. In SJJ, out TIV/TGD. Compare that pair against the reverse run in option 5 before anything else.
  • 3 · Croatia → Montenegro → Bosnia & Herzegovina — coast first, finish inland — Zagreb → Plitvice → Split → Dubrovnik → Kotor Bay and Perast → overland via Podgorica to Mostar or Sarajevo. In ZAG, out SJJ. The coast gets the lion's share, Bosnia the closing nights — and the longest internal leg comes last.
  • 4 · Bosnia & Herzegovina → Montenegro → Croatia — inland to the bay, then up the coast — Sarajevo → Mostar → Kotor Bay, Perast and Lovćen → Dubrovnik → Split → Zagreb. In SJJ, out ZAG — fly out of the end we arrive at. The bay sits in the middle and the Croatian coast is the finish.
  • 5 · Montenegro → Croatia → Bosnia & Herzegovina — the whole thing northbound — Tivat or Podgorica in → Kotor Bay and Lovćen → Dubrovnik → Split → Mostar → Sarajevo. In TIV/TGD, out SJJ. Same spine as option 2 run backwards; price it if the long-haul fares prefer the Adriatic end.
  • 6 · Montenegro → Bosnia & Herzegovina → Croatia — the reverse of option 1 — Tivat or Podgorica in → Kotor Bay and Lovćen → overland via Podgorica to Mostar and Sarajevo → Zagreb. In TIV/TGD, out ZAG. Worth exactly one look if the fares invert.

That's all six orders of the three countries on one list. The rule that shapes every one of them: one direction, open-jaw at the two ends, and nights spent rather than moved. What picks the winner is the flight maths — gateways, connections and timing — plus the fact that the Bosnia deck still needs a budget before any order giving it real nights can be compared properly. Once the order is chosen, the day-by-day gets rebuilt around the same principles.

Bosnia: day trips, or a trip of its own

Bosnia is in. The question is how — as day trips bolted onto a coastal route, or as the backbone of a Bosnia-first one (see the routes above). The day-trip options, ranked by how well they fit a relaxed trip:

Best fit

1 · Trebinje — half-day from Dubrovnik

30–45 min over the border. Ottoman-era old town, plane trees, a famous wine region, almost no coach traffic. Doable as a late-afternoon/evening trip so our Dubrovnik day stays intact.

If we only do one Bosnia thing, do this.

The classic

2 · Mostar — full day from Dubrovnik

Organised day tours run ~10–11h with the Stari Most, bazaar, often Počitelj or Blagaj. The catch: it eats one of our two full Dubrovnik days — walls, Lokrum and Mostar don't all fit in 3 nights.

Nearly free

3 · Neum — transit stop

Bosnia's 20km of coast, right on the Dubrovnik→Kotor road. Cheaper food and drink, a small beach. Useful as a swim-and-lunch break on the Kotor transfer.

Don't try to "do Mostar on the way" between Split and Dubrovnik in one day — you'd arrive with luggage at 7pm having seen the bridge for three rushed hours. Bosnia is in on every order; the point is that Mostar gets a night if it matters to the group, not a three-hour look between buses. All six leave room for that.

For the orders that give Bosnia real nights (1, 2, 4, 5 and 6), the deck's own 11-night plan is the detail to draw on: Sarajevo (4 nights) → Konjic & Mostar (4) → Jajce (2) → Sarajevo (1) — Baščaršija and the Latin Bridge, the Tunnel of Hope and War Childhood museums, Vrelo Bosne's springs, the Stari Most and Kujundžiluk bazaar, Blagaj, Počitelj and the Kravica waterfalls, rafting on the Neretva, and the Fortica skywalk above Mostar — then its suggestion to extend south into Montenegro (Kotor Bay, Perast, Lovćen). Jajce is a loop north of Sarajevo, so it only fits an order with room to double back inside Bosnia. The deck's budget slide carries no numbers yet; those get filled in before those orders can be compared properly.

Book early & get ready

Book early — late May is already busy:

  • Dubrovnik & Kotor accommodation — cruise traffic peaks, prices climb fast after mid-May.
  • Lake Skadar tours — they sell out in June.
  • Fixed-date items: our two flights.

Money & paperwork:

  • Currencies: Croatia = euro · Montenegro = euro (unilateral) · Bosnia = convertible mark (KM, pegged to the euro). Cards fine in Croatia and Montenegro; carry some cash in Bosnia.
  • Schengen: Croatia counts against your 90/180 days; Bosnia and Montenegro don't — both are visa-free with the Croatian Schengen visa in hand.
  • Passport: most nationalities need 3+ months validity beyond departure — check yours, this is the #1 trip-killer.
  • Insurance: an EHIC/GHIC won't cover Bosnia or Montenegro — get real travel insurance.
