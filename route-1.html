Route 1 27 May – 12 June 2027 16 nights 4 fixed in Zagreb Suggested split, not a booking

The straight line south: Zagreb, then the Dalmatian coast at full strength, a short Bosnia chapter at Mostar, and the Bay of Kotor to finish. It is the least awkward of the three to travel — one direction, no doubling back — and it spends the most nights on the sea.

The suggested split

Twelve route nights after Zagreb. This one gives the coast six of them and Bosnia two, with Kotor as the bay base and a final night by the airport.

NightsBaseDatesWhy here
4Zagreb27–30 MayFixed. Upper Town, St Mark's, Dolac, and a day for the museums or a slow start.
3Split31 May–2 JunDiocletian's palace, Marjan, and one island day. Plitvice is a stop on the way down, not a night.
3Dubrovnik3–5 JunWalls early, Srđ at sunset, and a day for the old town without a ferry to catch.
2Mostar6–7 JunStari Most, Blagaj, Počitelj and Kravica — Bosnia gets a short, full chapter.
3Kotor8–10 JunThe bay, Perast and Lovćen, with one day left unscheduled for the water.
1Podgorica11 JunA bed near the airport, not a sightseeing stop. The flight is the next evening.
16Total27 May–11 JunTwelve route nights plus the four fixed in Zagreb; fly out the evening of 12 June.

Getting between them

Patterns, not timetables — re-check six to eight weeks out. Times are typical daytime journeys, and every leg here fits inside a day.

No.LegTypicalHow
1Zagreb → Split6h30 trainDaytime train, or about 4h on the A1 if we have a car. A Plitvice stop makes it a long day; sleep in Split that night.
2Split → Dubrovnik~4h30 busThe coastal bus crosses the Neum corridor. The seasonal catamaran is faster but runs less often.
3Dubrovnik → Mostar~3h busInland via Trebinje — one border, then the Bosnian valley road. Go early; the bazaar is best in the late afternoon.
4Mostar → Kotor~5h busThe longest leg: back through Trebinje and over the Montenegrin border to the bay. If the direct bus isn't running, go via Dubrovnik.
5Kotor → Podgorica~2h busAround the bay, then inland. Keep the last afternoon free for the flight.

What it buys

  • Six coast nights. Split and Dubrovnik get real time, not day trips from a single base.
  • The simplest geometry. One direction south, no backtracking, and no transfer longer than about five hours.
  • Kotor at three nights. Enough for Perast, Lovćen and a slow bay day without moving again.
  • Bosnia still gets a chapter. Two nights at Mostar, with Blagaj and Počitelj as day trips.

What it costs

  • The priciest beds. Split and Dubrovnik are the two dearest places to sleep on the trip.
  • No Sarajevo. Bosnia is Mostar only; the inland capital doesn't feature at all.
  • Plitvice is squeezed. It becomes a stop on a transfer day, or gets dropped.
  • No Budva. Kotor is the only bay base, so the beaches are a day trip at best.

Still open

  • Whether Plitvice earns a night. It would have to come out of Split or Dubrovnik, and it changes the Zagreb–Split day completely.
  • Whether three nights in Kotor is one too many. If Budva is also wanted, one of these nights moves.
  • Car or bus for Zagreb–Split. The train is the easy default; a car only pays if Plitvice and the back roads are in the plan.
  • Accommodation and exact timetables. Nothing is booked, and the coastal catamaran schedule needs a fresh check.
  • The budget. Coast beds are the variable, and the Bosnia deck still carries no figures.
