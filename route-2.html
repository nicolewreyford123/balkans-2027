The inland opening: one long day to Sarajevo, four nights to settle, then Mostar and the coast before the bay. It gives Bosnia seven of the twelve route nights and the cheapest beds — at the price of the biggest single transfer at the start.

The suggested split

Twelve route nights after Zagreb. Bosnia takes seven of them; the coast and the bay share the rest, with Dubrovnik reduced to a two-night stop.

Getting between them

Patterns, not timetables — re-check six to eight weeks out. The opening haul is the outlier; after Sarajevo the legs are short.

No.LegTypicalHow
1Zagreb → Sarajevo~8h busThe long one. No useful train; an early bus gets in for dinner. Break it at Jajce only if Jajce gets a night, which this split doesn't give it.
2Sarajevo → Mostar2h trainThe Neretva line is the scenic one. Twice daily, so book the morning.

4Dubrovnik → Kotor~3h busThe coastal road through the Neum corridor, then the Debeli Brijeg crossing into Montenegro. The bay opens up after Herceg Novi.
5Kotor → Podgorica~2h busAround the bay, then inland. Keep the last afternoon free for the flight.

What it buys

  • Bosnia at the centre. Seven nights — Sarajevo four, Mostar three — the only route that gives Sarajevo a proper stay.
  • The cheapest beds. Sarajevo and Mostar undercut Split and Dubrovnik, so the money goes further.
  • Short legs after the start. Once the opening haul is done, the transfers run 2h, 3h, 3h, 2h.
  • Mostar at three nights. Enough for Blagaj, Počitelj and a river day without rushing.

What it costs

  • The trip opens with an eight-hour bus. That is a lost day compared with route 1's train to Split.
  • The Dalmatian coast is thin. Two nights in Dubrovnik, and no Split at all.
  • Kotor gets two nights. Lovćen and Perast end up competing for the same day.
  • It is the least beachy option. The coast is a stop on the way to the bay, not the point.

Still open

  • Whether the Zagreb–Sarajevo day is worth breaking at Jajce. It would cost a Bosnia night and turn one haul into two.
  • Whether two nights in Dubrovnik is enough. If not, the extra night has to come from Sarajevo or Mostar.
  • Car or bus for the opening haul. A car makes Jajce and the back roads possible; a bus keeps it simple.
  • Accommodation and exact timetables. The Sarajevo train and bus patterns need a fresh check before booking.
  • The budget. Bosnia's beds are cheap, but the deck still carries no figures.
