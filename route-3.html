Sarajevo inland

Route 3 · Croatia → Bosnia & Herzegovina → Montenegro · Zagreb in, Podgorica out

Route 3 27 May – 12 June 2027 16 nights 4 fixed in Zagreb Suggested split, not a booking

Compare: Route 1 · Coast first · Route 2 · Bosnia first · all three on the map

The most varied line, and the only one that doubles back: Split, then Mostar and Sarajevo inland, then back down to Kotor and Budva. It buys both Bosnian cities and a beach finish; it pays with the longest internal transfer and the most moves.

The fixed ends

In

Thu 27 May · Zagreb

JNB 00:35 → ZAG 19:35, Turkish via Istanbul (19h). An evening arrival, then four nights.

Fixed block

Thu 27 – Sun 30 May · Zagreb

In Zagreb through Sunday 30 May — four nights and three full days. No departure before the morning of Mon 31 May.

Out

Sat 12 June · Podgorica

TGD 20:45 → JNB 22:40 next day. The last night is in Podgorica, not on the coast.

16 nights, fixed at both ends: 4 in Zagreb + 12 on the route. The split below is a suggested shape, not a booking — what this page is testing is the order and where the nights fall.

The suggested split

Twelve route nights across seven bases — the most moves of the three. Budva gets the only three-night beach block, and the two Bosnian cities get two nights each.

What it buys

  • Both Bosnian cities. Sarajevo and Mostar each get a proper overnight; no other route does both.
  • A beach finish. Three nights in Budva, with Sveti Stefan and the riviera on the doorstep.
  • The most variety per night. Palace, bazaar, bridge, bay, beach — five different kinds of place.
  • Split still features. The palace and Marjan fit into two nights, even if the island day shrinks.

What it costs

  • It backtracks. Split → Mostar → Sarajevo → Kotor is a long inland loop, not a line south.
  • Seven bases in twelve route nights. The most packing and unpacking of the three.
  • Two nights almost everywhere. Only Budva gets three; the rest are tastes rather than stays.
  • The Sarajevo–Kotor day. About eight hours on a bus — the least pleasant transfer on any of the routes.

Still open

  • Whether Sarajevo is worth the backtrack. If Mostar alone is enough, that is route 1 without the long day.
  • Whether Budva's three nights should shrink to two. That would give Sarajevo or Split a third night.
  • How to run Sarajevo–Kotor. A direct bus, a split via Podgorica, or a local flight — each changes the day.
  • Accommodation and exact timetables. More bases means more bookings; the long leg needs the most re-checking.
  • The budget. More one-night stays and more transfers add up, and the Bosnia deck still carries no figures.
